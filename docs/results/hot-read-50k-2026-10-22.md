# Hot reads with 50,000 in flight

Load protection ([design](../design/load-protection.md), [guide](../load-protection.md)): one 1 MB
value read 100,000 times with **50,000 reads in flight**, with and without load protection, on
the two read paths: services reading through an embedded member, and smart clients.

## Setup

- `HotReadAllocation` (`benchmarks/`), one JVM per run: two members and the readers, `-Xmx8g`,
  JDK 25.0.5, a shared 4-core VM with 15 GB of memory, loopback networking.
- 1,000 warm-up reads, then 100,000 measured reads with `getAsync`, at most 50,000 in flight.
- Load protection on means `LoadProtectionConfig#setEnabled(true)` with the defaults and the map
  immutable (`MapConfig#setImmutable(true)`, `BINARY`).
- Clients: 64 smart clients (about 780 reads in flight each), in the same JVM as the members.
- Reads refused with `HecatonOverloadException` are retried after 10 ms, as an application that
  backs off would.

```sh
java -Xmx8g -XX:TLABSize=64k -XX:-ResizeTLAB ... -cp benchmarks/target/benchmarks.jar \
     uk.org.hecaton.benchmarks.fanout.HotReadAllocation --caller member --load-protection \
     --value-size 1m --concurrency 50000 --gets 100000 --warm-up-gets 1000
```

(`tools/perf/hot-read-allocation.sh` lists the JVM options.)

## Services reading through an embedded member

The hot key is owned by `member-0`; the reads go through `member-1`'s map proxy, as a service
embedding a member reads a key another member owns.

| Run | Result | Reads/s | Copies of the value per read on the members | GCs during the reads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hazelcast CE 5.7 | **Out of memory** (8 GB) within 30 s | — | 4 per `get`, 5 per `getAsync` (measured at 256 in flight) | — |
| Hecaton, load protection off | **Out of memory** (8 GB) within 20 s | — | the same | — |
| Hecaton, load protection on | Completed | 128,044 | 0 (25 bytes allocated per read) | 0 |
| Hecaton, load protection on, cold: all 50,000 reads are first reads of the key | Completed | 97,294 | 0: one remote read for all 50,000 (43 bytes per read) | 0 |

With load protection on, `member-1` reads the value from `member-0` once and its readers share it
(single-flight near cache of the immutable map). The reads/s are those of a local cache: no read
after the first crosses the network.

## Smart clients

| Run | Result | Reads/s | Copies per read on the members | Member bytes per read | Overload refusals |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hazelcast CE 5.7 | Completed | 892 | 0 | 3 KB | — |
| Hazelcast CE 5.7, client socket buffers 32 KB (the 5.7 stop-gap) | Completed | 1,011 | 0 | 3 KB | — |
| Hecaton, TLS, load protection off | Completed | 705 | 0 | 83 KB (TLS) | — |
| Hecaton, TLS, load protection on, first version | **Stalled**: connections closed as slow consumers, a read timed out | — | — | — | — |
| Hecaton, TLS, load protection on, fixed | Completed | 628 | 0 | 124 KB (TLS) | 1,228 |

In every client run the JVM's heap was full (8 GB in use after GC, 350–380 collections). The 64
clients share the JVM with the members and each holds the 1 MB values it receives, so this
harness cannot tell how much of the heap the members used; that needs the clients in other
JVMs or on other hosts.

### The stall, and its fix

The first version of load protection set the kernel's socket buffers of client connections to
`client-buffer-bytes` (32 KB), below one TCP segment on loopback (64 KB). Once a connection
reached its outbound limit, the member stopped reading it; the member's kernel held one full
segment for the client, the client had read everything that had arrived (`/proc/net/tcp`: member
send queue 65,764 bytes, client receive queue 0), and neither side sent again. After 30 s the
member closed the connections as slow consumers; clients that reconnected through the other
member left a member-to-member read without a response for 120 s. With 128 KB kernel buffers the
same run completed, and with buffer pooling off it still stalled, so the pool was not the cause.

The fix keeps the kernel buffers at the `hecaton.socket.client.*` sizes and uses
`client-buffer-bytes` only for the buffers in the heap
(`LoadProtectionNetworkTest#clientSocketsKeepTheKernelBufferSizes`). Hazelcast 5.7 with 32 KB
client socket buffers completed the same run: without load protection nothing pauses reads.

## Not measured here

- 50,000 client **connections**: this host cannot run them; the runs above have 50,000 reads in
  flight over 64 connections.
- The members' heap in the client runs (see above), TLS on the Hazelcast runs (not in CE), p99
  latency, connection churn and the 24-hour soak from the design's exit criteria.
